Opening

We’re all living with the same cross‑device reality: your macOS build can feel tactile and responsive on a Force Touch trackpad, then go completely mute on a desktop behind a KVM. Teams are finally treating haptics as a capability that can vanish at runtime, not a constant. This week is about building that mindset into your architecture, not chasing “works on my laptop.”

This Week's Big Story

Designing Core Haptics for macOS Apps

Your Mac app can’t assume a motor exists, stays warmed, or even belongs to your device. Capability checks, engine lifecycle, and usable fallbacks are product decisions, not just API calls. If you’ve shipped “random” no‑vibes bugs you can’t reproduce in CI, this piece shows you where determinism comes from.

Swift Snippet of the Week

This is capability-first thinking baked into an enum: route haptics per platform at runtime and you avoid “works on trackpad, fails on mouse” regressions without scattering #ifs and guesswork.
