Opening

Shaping a shipped macOS surface into SwiftUI is rarely a straight rewrite — it surfaces subtle runtime mismatches that only show up under real user workloads. Responder-chain timing, first-responder changes, and scroll-driven re-renders are the kinds of regressions that kill trust in a rollout. This issue focuses on a low-risk pattern to contain those problems while you migrate.

This Week's Big Story

Migrating AppKit Views to SwiftUI on macOS

Converting an AppKit-driven UI to SwiftUI often reveals differences in event delivery and render timing that don't exist in the simulator or in small samples. Scroll-heavy screens are the common pain point: excessive re-renders and misplaced first-responder behavior show up under load. The pragmatic approach below gives you a way to migrate incrementally without regressing scroll performance.

Swift Snippet of the Week

@MainActor final class FeedModel: ObservableObject { @Published var items = [String]() } let hosting = NSHostingView(rootView: FeedView(model: feedModel))

This pattern centralizes state across the AppKit/SwiftUI boundary so you avoid duplicated updates and reduce re-render surface area during scroll-heavy interactions.
